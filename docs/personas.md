# Preliminary personas

Status: draft hypotheses, not research findings. These fictional personas summarise anticipated needs; they must be tested with representative users before being presented as validated personas in the thesis.

## P-01 — Emma: first-time budget planner

Emma has a regular income but no structured budget. She finds manual categorisation time-consuming and is unsure how much she can spend after bills.

- Goal: create an understandable budget with minimal manual work.
- Needs: account selection, reviewable suggestions, clear categories and a simple monthly overview.
- Pain points: setup effort, unfamiliar budget concepts and uncertainty about remaining spending capacity.
- Relevant stories: US-01, US-02, US-03, US-04 and US-08.

## P-02 — Daniel: proactive budget planner

Daniel already tracks expenses but struggles with irregular bills and changes in everyday spending. A positive account balance can hide upcoming financial pressure.

- Goal: understand spending changes and identify potential shortfalls before they occur.
- Needs: dated payments, comparable spending periods, explained forecasts and actionable warnings.
- Pain points: annual bills, unexplained category changes and forecasts whose assumptions are unclear.
- Relevant stories: US-03, US-04, US-05, US-06, US-07 and US-08.

## Validation questions

1. How do participants currently plan bills and everyday spending?
2. Which parts of creating a budget require the most effort?
3. Can they distinguish account balance, budgeted spending and forecast balance?
4. Which information helps them understand and trust a warning?
5. Do separate budgets and the one-budget-per-account rule fit their needs?

Record participant evidence, contradictions and resulting design changes. Do not infer demographics or financial vulnerability from these fictional examples.
